    From 1,150 °C to your table

    Every vessel goes through the same five stages. The whole thing takes about twenty minutes of work and fourteen hours of cooling.

    1. 1,150 °C

      Gather

      We dip the blowpipe into the furnace and turn it to collect a honey-thick glob of molten glass.

    2. 1,000 °C

      Marver

      The gather is rolled on a steel table to centre it and cool its skin, so it holds a bubble.

    3. 900 °C

      Blow

      One long breath opens the bubble. Colour is added here, rolled in as powdered glass.

    4. 750 °C

      Shape

      Jacks, wooden paddles and wet newspaper pull out the neck and open the lip.

    5. 480 °C to room

      Anneal

      The piece cools slowly in a kiln overnight so it never cracks from stress. Then we sign it.

    Three people, two furnaces, one rule

    Ines, Tomás and Mira run the furnaces five days a week. The rule: nothing leaves the studio unless the person who blew it would put it on their own table. Seconds are recycled into the next gather.
